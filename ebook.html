Chamomile

History & Traditional Use

Mei learned chamomile late in her travels, in the kitchen gardens of Europe — though it hardly needed introducing. Few herbs have been welcomed into as many homes on as many continents. The ancient Egyptians honored it, European households made it the default evening cup for centuries, and it crossed the ocean with settlers who refused to leave it behind.

Traditionally Used For

Chamomile has traditionally been used to support relaxation, calm, and an easier drift toward sleep, and its gentle tea has long been a companion for an unsettled stomach.

How to Use

Steep one to two teaspoons of dried flowers in a cup of hot (not boiling) water, covered, for five to ten minutes. Covering the cup matters — it keeps the gentle aromatic oils in your tea instead of the air.

Good to Know

Chamomile belongs to the same family as ragweed; if you have that allergy, approach with care. It may add to the effect of sedative medications, and anyone pregnant, breastfeeding, or on blood thinners should speak with a healthcare provider first.
